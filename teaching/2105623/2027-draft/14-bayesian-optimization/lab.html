A hidden reactor yield, and a schedule to test

About 40 minutes, on a laptop or a phone. Everything runs on this page.

How this lab works

Part A looks for the best conditions of a reactor that you can only run, not see: each run gives a noisy yield, and you have 20 runs. A grid, random search and Bayesian optimization (BO) each spend the 20 runs; you compute one expected improvement by hand, follow BO run by run, and then see the hidden yield map. Part B tests a batch-plant schedule drafted by a language model, which contains three planted errors. Part C asks you to explain both. Write each prediction before you look; the next step stays locked until you do. Numbers are accepted within about 1% unless stated; after two tries the expected value is shown.

What runs where. The RUN steps, and the slider, show results that bo_lab.py computed in advance, or a small calculation that this page does in JavaScript; no Python is installed or run on your device. To run the Python yourself, open the slides and press D on a slide marked “D · Python” (it runs in your browser), or run bo_lab.py in desktop Python.

Studying on your own because class was cancelled? Watch the narrated slides for Session 14 first, then come back here. The runs on this page were recorded with NumPy and SciPy (bo_lab.py); the maps are recomputed in your browser from the recorded runs.

Part A · The reactor (synthetic kinetics, hidden from you)

A CSTR makes product B from A. You choose the temperature, 60 to 120 °C, and the residence time, 1 to 60 min (searched on a log scale). You measure the yield of B, in % of the A fed. A repeated run gives a different yield: the noise has a standard deviation of about 1.5 points. Each run takes a day, and the budget is 20 runs.

  • Grid: 4 temperatures (60, 80, 100, 120 °C) × 5 residence times (1, 2.8, 7.7, 21.6, 60 min)
  • Random search: 20 runs at random in the box
  • BO: 6 runs in a Latin hypercube, then 14 runs, each at the maximum of expected improvement (EI) from a Gaussian process (Matérn 5/2, ℓ and noise by the marginal likelihood)
  • Every method recommends its run with the best observed yield

Part B · A schedule drafted by a language model

A batch plant makes three products in two reactors over five days. A batch of product p in reactor r makes a fixed amount, takes a fixed time and has a fixed cost. Each reactor has 20 h a day. P3 cannot be made in R1. Product due on a day must be in stock at the end of that day; stock costs 20 USD per tonne per day. The model chooses the number of batches (integers) and the stock. This specification was given to a language model, which drafted the model. The draft contains three planted errors: one in units, one in an index, and one invented data entry.
