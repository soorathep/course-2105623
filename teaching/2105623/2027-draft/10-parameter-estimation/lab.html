Rate constants of α-pinene isomerization

About 40 minutes. Works on a laptop or a phone; nothing to install.

How this lab works

Heated α-pinene turns into four products. From eight samples of the five weight fractions you estimate five first-order rate constants by least squares, judge how well each is determined, look for dependencies in the data, and decide which measurements and which sampling times would help most. All fits were run in advance with SciPy (least_squares, Levenberg–Marquardt) on the model below; you read and test the results. Write each prediction before you look; the next step stays locked until you do. Numbers are accepted within about 2% unless stated; after two tries the expected value is shown.

Studying on your own because class was cancelled? Watch the narrated slides for Session 10 first, then come back here. The numbers on this page were made with SciPy 1.14.1, the version that runs in the slides' browser Python.

The data (Fuguitt and Hawkins, 1947; as analyzed by Box, Hunter, MacGregor and Erjavec, 1973)

dy₁/dt = −(θ₁ + θ₂)y₁, dy₂/dt = θ₁y₁, dy₃/dt = θ₂y₁ − (θ₃ + θ₄)y₃ + θ₅y₅, dy₄/dt = θ₃y₃, dy₅/dt = θ₄y₃ − θ₅y₅, with y(0) = (100, 0, 0, 0, 0) weight % and θ in 1/min. The equations are linear, so y(t) = eKty(0), computed with scipy.linalg.expm. Rate constants are reported in 10⁻⁵ 1/min.
