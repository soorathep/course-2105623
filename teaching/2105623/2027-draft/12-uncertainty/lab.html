Capacity, risk and a fuel blend under uncertainty

About 40 minutes. Everything runs on this page, on a laptop or a phone.

How this lab works

Part A sizes two production lines before the demands are known and compares the mean-value plan with the stochastic plan. Part B adds a CVaR term and asks what protection in the bad cases costs. Part C blends a low-sulfur fuel oil when the sulfur of one cargo is uncertain: nominal, chance-constrained and robust. Part D asks you to explain. Write each prediction before you look; the next step stays locked until you do. Numbers are accepted within about 1% unless stated; after two tries the expected value is shown.

What runs where. The RUN and CHANGE steps, and the sliders, show results that unc_lab.py computed in advance, or a small calculation that this page does in JavaScript; no Python is installed or run on your device. To run the Python yourself, open the slides and press D on a slide marked “D · Python” (it runs in your browser), or run unc_lab.py in desktop Python.

Studying on your own because class was cancelled? Watch the narrated slides for Session 12 first, then come back here. The recorded LP solutions on this page were made with SciPy 1.14.1 and HiGHS, the versions that run in the slides' browser Python.

The plant (synthetic data)

Two products. A dedicated line makes only A and costs 170 USD per day for each t/d of capacity. A flexible line makes A or B and costs 200 USD per day per t/d; one t/d of B uses 1.25 t/d of flexible capacity. Margins before capacity cost: A 300 USD/t, B 450 USD/t. Demand that cannot be met is lost. Capacity is built now; production is chosen after the demands are known.

The demands move in opposite directions: when A is high, B tends to be low. Means: A 90 t/d, B 40 t/d.

100 t of marine fuel at least cost, with sulfur ≤ 0.50 wt% (the IMO 2020 global limit) and a viscosity blending number VBN ≤ 34 (linear by mass).
