Units, scaling and three algorithms

About 40 minutes. Works in class or on your own, on a laptop or a phone. Nothing to install; a calculator helps.

How this lab works

A two-stage compressor with an intercooler has two decisions: the intermediate pressure and the gas temperature after the cooler. You run steepest descent, Newton's method and BFGS on the annual cost with the variables in SI units, find out why one method stalls, rescale, and run again. Then you look at where the optimum lies, and count the work of each method on the Rosenbrock function. Write each prediction before you look; the next step stays locked until you do. Numbers are accepted within about 1%; after two tries the expected value is shown.

Studying on your own because class was cancelled? Watch the narrated slides for Session 8 first, then come back here.

The compressor (synthetic)

QuantityValue
Gas flow n, ideal gas with γ = 1.450 mol/s
Inlet: p₁, T₁1 bar, 300 K
Outlet of stage 2: p₃10 bar
Isentropic efficiency of each stage η0.80
Intercooler: U; cooling water in, out40 W/m²K; 293 K, 303 K
Electricity; cooler area (annualized); cooling water0.8 USD/yr per W; 300 USD/yr per m²; 0.03 USD/yr per W removed

Decisions: x = (p, T), the intermediate pressure and the gas temperature leaving the intercooler. With a = (γ − 1)/γ and cp = γR/(γ − 1) = 29.1 J/mol·K: stage 1 heats the gas to Tout,1 = T₁ + T₁[(p/p₁)a − 1]/η; power W = n cp{T₁[(p/p₁)a − 1] + T[(p₃/p)a − 1]}/η; cooler duty Q = n cp(Tout,1 − T); area A = Q/(U·ΔTlm) with countercurrent water.

Minimize f(p, T) = 0.8 W + 300 A + 0.03 Q in USD/yr. No rows: T stays above the water inlet on its own, because ΔTlm → 0 there and the area cost grows without bound. Gradients by complex step; every run starts at (5 bar, 340 K) and stops when max|∂f/∂xi| ≤ 10⁻³ or after 5,000 iterations.
