Sharing feed between parallel reactors

About 40 minutes. Works in class or on your own, on a laptop or a phone. Nothing to install; a calculator helps.

How this lab works

Three CSTRs in parallel share one feed. A reactor makes more B per m³ of feed when it gets less feed, because the residence time is longer, so the plant must decide how to split the feed. You predict which limits bind, solve, check the KKT conditions yourself, then read the multipliers as values and test them by re-solving. Write each prediction before you look; the next step stays locked until you do. Numbers are accepted within about 1%; after two tries the expected value is shown.

What runs where. The RUN and CHANGE steps, and the slider, show results that kkt_lab.py computed in advance, or a small calculation that this page does in JavaScript; no Python is installed or run on your device. To run the Python yourself, open the slides and press D on a slide marked “D · Python” (it runs in your browser), or run kkt_lab.py in desktop Python.

First-order reaction A → B in each CSTR. Feed: 15 m³/h with CA0 = 2 kmol/m³. B made in reactor i: Pi = CA0FikiVi/(Fi + kiVi) kmol/h. R1 releases 80 MJ per kmol of B and can remove 125 kW of heat; R3 needs at least 2 m³/h of circulation.

In the course form: minimize −ΣPi subject to h = ΣFi − 15 = 0 (λ), gcool = 22.22·P1 − 125 ≤ 0 (μcool, kW), Fi − Fmax,i ≤ 0 and Fmin,i − Fi ≤ 0 (μ ≥ 0). Since P1 rises with F1, the cooling row is the same as an upper bound on F1; the problem is convex.
