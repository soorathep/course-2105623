How this lab works

Part A finds the best temperature for a batch reactor, first constant, then as a profile by single shooting. Part B solves the same problem by collocation with Ipopt in Colab and compares. Part C controls the level of two tanks by MPC, with and without a disturbance estimate, and tunes it. Part D asks what happens to the batch profile when a rate constant is wrong. Write each prediction before you look; the next step stays locked until you do. Numbers are accepted within about 2% unless stated; after two tries the expected value is shown.

The batch reactor (Logsdon and Biegler, 1989)

A → B → C: dx₁/dt = −k₁x₁², dx₂/dt = k₁x₁² − k₂x₂, with k₁ = 4000 exp(−2500/T) and k₂ = 620,000 exp(−5000/T) in 1/h, x(0) = (1, 0). The temperature T(t) may be chosen between 298 and 398 K. Maximize x₂, the fraction of B, at t = 1 h.

Single shooting on this page: T is constant on each of N equal intervals; each evaluation integrates the batch with classical RK4 and a step of 0.005 h; SLSQP chooses the N temperatures with finite-difference gradients.

Part C · Level control of two tanks by MPC


A₁ dh₁/dt = u − h₁/R₁, A₂ dh₂/dt = h₁/R₁ − h₂/R₂ + d, with A = 1 m², R₁ = 2 and R₂ = 4 min/m², valve flow 0 ≤ u ≤ 0.5 m³/min, sample time 0.5 min. MPC controls h₂: setpoint 1.2 → 1.6 m at 5 min; an unmeasured withdrawal d = −0.05 m³/min from tank 2 starts at 30 min. Each sample MPC solves min Σ(h₂ − r)² + w Σ(Δu)² over N samples with 0 ≤ u ≤ 0.5, by bounded least squares (scipy.optimize.lsq_linear).
